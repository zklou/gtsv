Where the LiDAR reaches

Every beam of the sensor’s published pattern, cast against the same van, camera housing and street as the review page. A return only counts if the sensor’s range on that surface allows it. Pick a set-up; every figure follows.

One rotation, seen from above

Each dot is one return on the ground or a kerb face, from a single 0.1 s turn of the sensor, coloured by its distance from the LiDAR. The van drives to the right. Rings mark 5, 10, 20 and 30 m from the LiDAR on the ground. The lanes, kerbs and sidewalks are the ones drawn on the review page.

What a drive scans across the road, and from how close

A slice across the road, looking forward. As the van drives past, its beams sweep every point in this slice. Colour shows the closest distance from which any beam reaches that point. Close means dense, precise points; pale means the point is only seen from far away. Blank is never reached. Signs and a signal head at standard US heights are drawn for scale.

How this was computed

  • Beam layouts from the Ouster Rev8 datasheets: OS1 128 beams evenly from −22° to +22°, OS0 from −45° to +45°, 2048 columns per turn.
  • Mounts from the model: tilted post at 2.72 m, tipped 15° toward the road; old mast at 2.87 m, upright. The van, mirrors, A/C, camera housing, cameras and frame are the review page’s own geometry.
  • Range model: a surface of reflectivity ρ seen at incidence i returns out to the datasheet 10 % range × √(ρ cos i / 0.1). Asphalt 12 %, concrete 30 %, grass 40 % at 865 nm. The slice assumes a 30 % surface facing the sensor, capped at 60 m.
  • The street is flat and straight, with nothing else on it. Parked cars, other traffic and trees would hide parts of what is shown.
  • Regenerate with npx tsx tools/lidar-sim.mts --chosen --coverage lidar-coverage.html in the gtsv repository.
